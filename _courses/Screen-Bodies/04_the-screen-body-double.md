## The Screen Body Double

Today we are looking at avatars, digital doppelgängers, bodies who live on screen, and the feedback loops between the screen bodies and physical bodies.

### Robbie Cooper, [*Alter Ego*](https://robbiecooper.com/project/alter-ego), 2007

![Alter Ego]({{ site.baseurl }}/courses/Screen-Bodies/assets/img/cooper.jpg)
<figcaption>Robbie Cooper, from <em>Alter Ego</em>, 2007</figcaption>

<blockquote><p>In 2003 I was photographing the CEO of a company, when he told me that he used virtual world games to play with his children. He was divorced and had bad access to them, so he would meet them every evening in "Everquest", where they would play and chat. I asked him, what did they talk about? He told me that they discussed things like homework, school, their mother; the normal stuff of humdrum reality. His description of this banal but emotionally important exchange, taking place in the vivid fantasy of the game, got me thinking about the nature of the game itself; it's a world of surface appearances and symbols. Within that, their interaction had been reduced to text; it was a technological extension of psychological models- the imaginary, and the symbolic structure of language.</p>
<p>Reading the work of the economist Edward Castronova, it was already obvious that these surface appearances were rapidly achieving significant real world value, as items for sale between players. Castronova's 2001 paper "Virtual Worlds: A First Hand Account of Market and Society on the Cyberian Frontier" now seems quaint in how far it underestimated the rate of growth in this secondary market. But the level of demand was already climbing at an extraordinary rate. I spent the next three years, on and off, travelling to places like Korea, China, France and Germany to photograph people who use virtual world games. By recording the appearance of the real person, alongside that of their avatar, I wanted to compare each person with the identity that they'd created to interact with others online.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://robbiecooper.com/project/alter-ego">robbiecooper.com/project/alter-ego</a></p></blockquote>

### [LaTurbo Avedon](https://www.laturboavedon.com/), *ID*, 2015

![LaTurbo Avedon]({{ site.baseurl }}/courses/Screen-Bodies/assets/img/laturbo.png)
<figcaption>LaTurbo Avedon, still from <em>ID</em>, 2015</figcaption>

<blockquote><p>LaTurbo Avedon is an artist and avatar whose work gives new meaning to the term “born-digital” – as Avedon is a gender-neutral figure that originated in, and works within, virtual spaces. In those spaces, found in video works and animations, they question the role of digital authorship and the boundary between physical and virtual worlds.</p>
<p>Through their “constant state of character creation,” Avedon explores technology’s transformation of personhood. There’s no trace of the artist behind the avatar as Avendon doesn't see a distinction between the two. They say, “My experience and identity is only connected to what I have done online. As a render, I am making my character not much different than most people, sharing pics, making things, and chatting via social media.”</p>
<p>Their latest work “Sittin’ Up in My Room” (2018) is an animated image series whose voyeuristic invitations into Avedon’s bedroom complicate our limited perceptions of what it means to live on the digital plane. Throughout their video and animation work, Avedon takes visual cues from the 3D world of Second Life, having remarked that they spend more time playing video games than surfing the web. Their ongoing project “New Sculpt” uses different game development applications like Unity to propose a non-material way of creating and exhibiting sculpture. Avedon also maintains an active d.rip feed, where they post artwork and interact with followers.</p>
<p>And while Avedon makes their own art, their very existence online – where they curate shows and deejay in virtual clubs – can be seen as an extended performance or a thought-experiment that seeks to provide newfound meaning to the digital world as a valid space in which humans can exist and interact. “While you may not ever meet me walking through the park or on the street somewhere,” they say, “my experiences in ... virtual places are lived and legitimate.”</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://www.artsy.net/artwork/laturbo-avedon-id">artsy.net/artwork/laturbo-avedon-id</a></p></blockquote>

### Robin Bervini, [*Until I Stop Trying To Get Out Of My Skin*](https://www.robinbervini.com/until-i-stop-trying-to-get-out-of-my-skin), 2020

![Robin Bervini]({{ site.baseurl }}/courses/Screen-Bodies/assets/img/bervini.webp)
<figcaption>Robin Bervini, from <em>Until I Stop Trying To Get Out Of My Skin</em>, 2020</figcaption>

<blockquote><p>Robin Bervini is the offspring of a bi-racial union. Being mixed race in Southern Switzerland, he always struggled in being perceived and accepted as Swiss. Because of this, growing up he has been negating his ethnicity to himself. He evaded his skin by embodying white avatars in video games, the only way he could live his white fantasy. Using that same strategy, this project takes the viewers into the artist’s skin trough a virtual reality experience and a spatial installation. After digitizing his entire body, the artist created alter-egos representing his ideal selves in different stages of life, while their identity is a manifestation of the artist’s own internalized racism, shame, social anxiety and lived discriminations.</p>
<p>The realization of this project involved the use of 3D scanning and photogrammetry techniques to digitize the artist’s body, and a series of processes to enable motion capture of facial performance and optimize the models as game-ready assets in order to create the virutal reality experience.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://www.robinbervini.com/until-i-stop-trying-to-get-out-of-my-skin">robinbervini.com/until-i-stop-trying-to-get-out-of-my-skin</a></p></blockquote>

### [Lu Yang](http://luyang.asia/), *DOKU Trilogy*, 2018 – 2025

![Lu Yang]({{ site.baseurl }}/courses/Screen-Bodies/assets/img/yang.webp)
<figcaption>Lu Yang, still from <em>DOKU The Creator</em>, 2025</figcaption>

<blockquote><p>Lu Yang’s ongoing DOKU trilogy of video works (2018–2025) explores the dissolution of selfhood in relation to digital, biological, and spiritual systems. Across DOKU The Self (2022), DOKU The Flow (2024) and DOKU The Creator (2025), the Chinese artist (*1984 in Shanghai, based in Shanghai/Tokyo) employs video game aesthetics, motion-capture technology, and Buddhist philosophy to interrogate where identity resides when consciousness can be rendered, replicated, and endlessly reborn.</p>
<p>Throughout the trilogy an avatar based on the artist’s own likeness is seen to experience or inhabit various realities, ranging from mundane to the utterly fantastic. Inspired by ideas from Yogācāra Buddhism—particularly the concept of ālayavijñāna or “storehouse consciousness”—Lu Yang pictures DOKU not as a stable character but as a flickering assemblage of data, neurons, and spiritual concepts. Altogether, the trilogy asks whether the self, once decomposed into genetic code, brain activity, and algorithmic behavior, can be said to exist at all, or whether it persists only as impermanent patterns cycling through biological and digital networks.</p>
<p>Source: <a href="https://www.fotoarsenalwien.at/en/exhibition/lu-yang-en">fotoarsenalwien.at/en/exhibition/lu-yang-en</a></p></blockquote>

### Yifan Li, [*What Is Your Favorite Primitive?*](https://drive.google.com/drive/search?q=yifan%20li), 2023

![Yifan Li]({{ site.baseurl }}/courses/Screen-Bodies/assets/img/li.jpg)
<figcaption>Yifan Li, still from <em>What Is Your Favorite Primitive?</em>, 2023</figcaption>

<blockquote><p>Li Yi-Fan is a contemporary artist who skillfully combines image-making technologies with narrative inquiry. He describes his creative process as a “death match” between the artist and software—a confrontation whose exhausted collision eventually gives rise to a quietly emerging narrative. Rather than relying on commercial tools, Li insists on building his own image-making systems from scratch, crafting a DIY yet technically advanced platform that rivals the capabilities of the resource-intensive digital effects industry. This autonomy reflects a distinct technological literacy and a deeply philosophical approach to image creation.</p>
<p>Li’s works often integrate game engines and real-time rendering technologies, allowing him to improvise intricate 3D animations with performative immediacy. What Is Your Favorite Primitive (2023), for example, takes the form of a satirical tech keynote presentation, in which a protagonist grapples with the ethical and societal questions embedded in software tools designed for image production. Through this work, Li reflects on how images reshape modes of communication and how emojis might transmit emotional meanings that surpass individual perception. At its core, the piece speculates whether image technologies might one day project an as-yet-unrealized totality—constructing a new politics of life and death.</p>
<p>Source: artist portfolio</p></blockquote>

### [Nina Davies](https://www.ninadavies.net/), [*Precursing*](https://artreview.com/art-lovers-movie-club-nina-davies-precursing-2023/), 2023

![Nina Davies]({{ site.baseurl }}/courses/Screen-Bodies/assets/img/davies.jpeg)
<figcaption>Nina Davies, still from <em>Precursing</em>, 2023</figcaption>

<blockquote><p>A little-known aspect of video games is that some serve as a training space for artificial intelligence. In Davies’ words, these people who imitate NPCs in public spaces are like ghosts between two worlds: they live in one of them, but have learned to communicate with the other.</p>

## Assignment

Choose one of the works from today's session and create a digital body double in response to it, presented as a series of 3 – 5 images. Your double can be a faithful representation of yourself, or a way of being someone else and escaping the constraints of a physical body.

You are free to develop your own approach. If you are looking for a starting point, here are some ideas:

- **Create an avatar:** build a digital version of yourself, or of someone you would like to be, and show it in different situations (Bervini, Lu Yang, Sato). We will make avatars together in class next week, so you can also start this option then
- **Screenshot a game avatar:** take screenshots of an avatar you play in a game or virtual world, ideally together with a photograph of the person behind it (Cooper)
- **Act like a digital body:** photograph bodies behaving like digital ones, for example imitating the movements and gestures of game characters in physical space (Davies)

Upload your images to the [shared doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/11vETXh-5hIMwRI7BrlrTQS5nLR35g1phnfOYlGiM31g/edit?tab=t.mh9jb7wlcs1p), together with a title and one sentence naming the work you responded to.

## Aufgabe

Wählt eines der Werke der heutigen Sitzung und entwickelt als Antwort darauf ein digitales Körper-Double, präsentiert als Serie aus 3 – 5 Bildern. Euer Double kann eine getreue Darstellung eurer selbst sein oder eine Möglichkeit, jemand anderes zu sein und den Beschränkungen eines physischen Körpers zu entkommen.

Ihr seid frei, euren eigenen Ansatz zu entwickeln. Falls ihr einen Ausgangspunkt sucht, hier einige Ideen:

- **Einen Avatar erstellen:** Baut eine digitale Version von euch selbst oder von einer Figur, die ihr gern wärt, und zeigt sie in verschiedenen Situationen (Bervini, Lu Yang, Sato). Wir erstellen nächste Woche gemeinsam im Kurs Avatare, ihr könnt diese Option also auch dann beginnen
- **Ein Spiel-Avatar im Screenshot:** Haltet einen Avatar, den ihr in einem Spiel oder einer virtuellen Welt spielt, in Screenshots fest, idealerweise zusammen mit einem Foto der Person dahinter (Cooper)
- **Wie ein digitaler Körper agieren:** Fotografiert Körper, die sich wie digitale verhalten, zum Beispiel indem sie die Bewegungen und Gesten von Spielfiguren im physischen Raum nachahmen (Davies)

Ladet eure Bilder in das [geteilte Dokument](https://docs.google.com/document/d/11vETXh-5hIMwRI7BrlrTQS5nLR35g1phnfOYlGiM31g/edit?tab=t.mh9jb7wlcs1p) hoch, zusammen mit einem Titel und einem Satz, der das Werk nennt, auf das ihr euch bezieht.
